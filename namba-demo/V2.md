Read `DEMO.md` first; it still holds (audience, story, brand rule, budgets,
zero console errors). v1 is live at https://zack-niv.github.io/namba-demo/.

## Contracts between agents (v2)

**Counters (Shops → Humans, Flow).** During `Shops.init()` (build phase, runs
before the crowd), publish:

```js
ctx.counters = [{
  slotId, name, kind,          // kind: 'cafe' | 'tempura' | 'food' | 'shop' | ...
  level,                        // level id e.g. '2F'
  staff: { x, y, z, yaw },      // where a staff member stands behind the counter (yaw: facing the customer)
  order: { x, y, z },           // walkable spot in front of the counter where the player stands to order
  role,                         // 'barista' | 'chef' | 'clerk'
}];
```

Every café/food place on or near the route gets one (Pine Tree Coffee, Sunny
Side Café, Wakakusa, Tempura Daikichi at minimum). The `order` spot must be on a
WALK cell so the player can reach it.

**Staff (Humans).** For each `ctx.counters` entry within draw range, render a
staff figure at `staff` (idle animation, uniform/apron); emit nothing. Humans
also expose `ctx.crowd.wave(x, z)` unchanged if it exists.

**Order (Flow).** In `js/game/interact.js`: near an `order` spot (≤ 1.6 m,
facing the counter), the E prompt reads *"Order a coffee ☕"* (cafés) /
*"Say hi"* (others). Pressing E plays a small, charming exchange
(caption: いらっしゃいませ! → "One latte, please." → a cup appears in the HUD
/ journal "☕ Pine Tree Coffee — latte"). Emits `demo:order {slotId}`. No
blocking modal; never more than ~3 s.

**Phone raise/lower (Phone ↔ Flow).** The phone owns the key binding and
emits `phone:pose {pose: 'up'|'glance'|'down'}`. Flow's controls walkthrough
(item 9) must teach the same keys — Phone writes the final binding in
`notes/v2-phone.md` early; Flow reads it.

## Quality bar per item

- **1** No pixel of the lower floor visible through any track pit, from any
  angle on the platform. Solid ballast bed + sleepers + pit walls, darker
  underneath. Check all Nankai tracks and the Midosuji/Sennichimae pits.
- **2** No formant babble anywhere. Use `speechSynthesis` with a real `ja-JP`
  voice for Japanese lines and an `en-*` voice for the English line that
  follows (that's how real Japanese PA works: JP then EN). If no voice is
  available, play the chime only and show the caption. Short, real phrases.
- **3** Train PA and arrival/departure sounds are loud on platforms, faint on
  the concourse just above, and silent elsewhere. Distinct beds per area type:
  platform (rumble, wind, PA), gate hall (IC beeps, footsteps), shopping
  arcade (BGM from shops, chatter), Namba CITY (mall music, softer), Parks
  canyon (open air, birds, wind, distant city), 6F dining (sizzle, clatter,
  irasshaimase). Cross-fade between them as you walk.
- **4** Every poster/ad readable and crisp at 1.5 m: no stretched or tiny
  canvases, correct aspect ratio, mipmaps + anisotropy, no NaN UVs.
- **5** People should read as people, not toys, at 2–10 m: proportioned
  bodies, real clothing silhouettes, natural walk cycle with arm swing.
  Recommended: rigged CC0 GLTF humans (e.g. Quaternius CC0 packs; any GitHub
  repo is reachable via `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/<owner>/<repo>@<ref>/<path>`,
  npm packages via `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/...`; `poly.pizza`, GitHub API
  and github.com are blocked). Licence must be CC0/CC-BY/MIT and recorded in
  `assets/humans/LICENSE.md`. Mixamo-derived files (three.js `Michelle`,
  `Xbot`, `Soldier`) are a fallback only. Performance: hybrid LOD (nearest
  ~30–40 as skinned meshes with an AnimationMixer, the rest instanced/baked),
  or vertex-animation textures. Total download ≤ ~6 MB, 60 fps at
  `quality=high`.
- **6** Cafés: counter with espresso machine, pastry case, menu boards, mixed
  seating (bar stools at the window, 2-tops, a sofa corner), plants, warm
  pendant lights. Deep rooms: put a back wall/kitchen so they're not cavernous.
  Restaurants, shops, bookstore: same thinking, believable layouts. Variety,
  not a grid.
- **8** No standing clumps in corridors or at the foot of escalators; agents
  yield to the player (sidestep early, never push through them), keep left on
  escalators, queue at ticket gates and counters, sit at café tables, stand
  near shop windows. Fewer, better-placed people beat many stacked ones.
- **9** At the start of the journey (after the title, before/with Aya's first
  text): a 3–4 card, skippable, in-world-styled walkthrough: move/look,
  run, interact (E), phone up/down, "follow signs, the map lies 🙃". Under
  12 s total if the player just clicks through.
- **10, 12** Lead with ONE big, plain next instruction ("Take the escalator up
  to 2F", with a distance and a simple arrow), a clear "You are here · 2F"
  and the destination. The 3D stack is secondary (tap to expand). Fewer
  numbers, bigger text, one glance = one decision.
- **11** The phone is down by default while walking: a slim glance strip at
  the bottom shows the next step. One key (or right-click hold) raises it to
  full view; it lowers when you move fast or after a few seconds of walking.
  Animate it like a real hand: lift, tilt, settle.
